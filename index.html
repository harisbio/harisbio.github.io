About

Bridging the bench and the terminal.

My work sits across health biology, biotechnology, and bioinformatics. Human disease is at the centre of it, so far cardiomyopathy and leukemia, but the same questions about gene regulation and stress response have also taken me into crop and environmental biology. Moving between these systems keeps sharpening the part I care about most: turning raw sequencing data into specific, testable hypotheses.

I trained at the bench first, so I think about how a computational result would be checked experimentally. I keep analyses reproducible, with documented pipelines built with Bash scripting, R, and Python, and when a workflow proves useful I turn it into a tool or database that others can use.

Cardiac disease genomics

What goes wrong outside the sarcomere in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy?

BS thesis, extended into a paper under review at Current Genomics; continued at single-cell resolution in my MS thesis.

Advisor Prof. Dr. Farrukh Azeem

1 / 8

HCM is the most common inherited heart disease, yet it is studied almost entirely through sarcomere mutations and treated mainly for its symptoms. I analysed myocardial RNA-seq to look for regulation outside the sarcomere, then used protein networks and drug-target databases to ask which of those changes existing drugs could reach.

Key findings
  • Zinc homeostasis is disrupted: the metallothioneins MT1A and MT1M are downregulated, pointing to impaired mineral absorption.
  • Innate immune signalling is dysregulated: FPR1 and FPR2 are downregulated and neutrophil extracellular trap pathways are enriched.
  • Hub genes: FPR1, FPR2, CXCR1, CCR1, SSTR5, EDN2, EDN3, S100A8, S100A9, and TLR2.
  • Repurposing leads: zinc salts (chloride, acetate, sulfate), the FPR antagonists rebamipide and nedocromil, and the S100A9 modulator tasquinimod.
  • Findings are computational and await experimental validation.

Which high-impact variants hide in acute myeloid leukemia exomes?

Summer research, ICCBS, Jamil-ur-Rahman Center for Genome Research, University of Karachi.

Advisors Prof. Dr. Shahid Mansoor Dr. Atia Gohar

Research synthesis & software

How has genome-wide gene-family analysis actually been done for 25 years?

Systematic review and meta-analysis with an open, searchable database of the screened literature; under review at Genome.

Advisor Prof. Dr. Muhammad Amjad Nawaz National Research Tomsk State University

1 / 8

Genome-wide gene-family studies number in the hundreds, but their methods and results are scattered. I screened four databases under PRISMA and extracted each included study into a structured dataset. Doing that by hand was not realistic, so I wrote the PDF extractor and the PGF-Explorer database listed under Software.

Key findings
  • Crops dominate: wheat (11.9%), rice (5.5%), and tomato (4.8%) lead the primary organisms.
  • WRKY is the most studied family (3.2% of studies).
  • Modern model-selection tools remain rare: most studies still build their trees in MEGA rather than IQ-TREE.
  • The average study team has 7.03 authors.
  • The field's main weaknesses are inconsistent reporting and a lack of methodological standardization, which the proposed pipeline addresses.

04

Environmental biotechnology

Can plant-made iron nanoparticles make textile wastewater safe for crops?

Joint study of the Departments of Bioinformatics and Biotechnology and Environmental Sciences, GCUF; under review at Plant, Cell & Environment.

Advisors Prof. Dr. Sabir Hussain Dr. Fatima Batool

1 / 12

I synthesised iron oxide nanoparticles with Conocarpus erectus leaf extract, compared them with chemically made ones, and used them to treat textile effluent before irrigating wheat. A public cadmium-stress wheat transcriptome then mapped the defence pathways wheat activates under cadmium stress.

Key findings
  • Leaf-extract nanoparticles outperformed chemically made ones at removing COD, the main organic pollution load.
  • Untreated effluent is severely toxic, cutting fresh and dry biomass by about 38 to 43%.
  • Stress triggers an active transcriptional response, not collapse: upregulated genes outnumber downregulated ones more than two to one.
  • Defence modules: glutathione metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, cell wall remodelling, and metal transporters, centred on GST and NAS genes.
