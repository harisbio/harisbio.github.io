About

Bridging the bench and the terminal.

My work sits across health biology, biotechnology, and bioinformatics. Human disease is at the centre of it, so far cardiomyopathy and leukemia, but the same questions about gene regulation and stress response have also taken me into crop and environmental biology. Moving between these systems keeps sharpening the part I care about most: turning raw sequencing data into specific, testable hypotheses.

I trained at the bench first, so I think about how a computational result would be checked experimentally. I keep analyses reproducible, with documented pipelines built with Bash scripting, R, and Python, and when a workflow proves useful I turn it into a tool or database that others can use.

HCM Transcriptomics

What goes wrong outside the sarcomere in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy?

BS thesis, extended into a paper under review at Current Genomics; continued at single-cell resolution in my MS thesis.

1 / 8

HCM is the most common inherited heart disease, yet it is studied almost entirely through sarcomere mutations and treated only for its symptoms. I analysed myocardial RNA-seq to look for regulation outside the sarcomere, then used protein networks and drug-target databases to ask which of those changes existing drugs could reach.

Key findings
  • Zinc homeostasis is disrupted: the metallothioneins MT1A and MT1M are downregulated, pointing to impaired mineral absorption.
  • Innate immunity is activated, including neutrophil extracellular trap formation.
  • Hub genes: FPR1, FPR2, CXCR1, CCR1, SSTR5, EDN2, EDN3, S100A8, S100A9, and TLR2.
  • Repurposing leads: zinc salts (chloride, acetate, sulfate), the FPR antagonists rebamipide and nedocromil, and the S100A9 modulator tasquinimod.
  • Findings are computational and await experimental validation.

Cancer genomics

Which high-impact variants hide in acute myeloid leukemia exomes?

Summer research, ICCBS, Jamil-ur-Rahman Center for Genome Research, University of Karachi, supervised by Prof. Dr. Shahid Mansoor.

1 / 7

Ten AML whole exomes from the Sequence Read Archive, processed end to end in Linux, Galaxy, and R: quality control, alignment to GRCh38, variant calling, functional annotation, and a novelty screen against dbSNP and ClinVar to isolate variants no database had reported.

Key findings
  • 399 putatively novel SNVs and indels, all predicted high impact, form a shortlist for functional and clinical validation.
  • Transitions outnumber transversions several fold, the expected mutational signature for exome data of this kind.
  • MUC19 is the most recurrently hit gene, with six high-impact variants; most other genes carry one or two.
  • Rainfall analysis flags clustered mutation regions below a 10 kb inter-mutation distance.

Research synthesis & software

How has genome-wide gene-family analysis actually been done for 25 years?

First author, PRISMA systematic review and meta-analysis under review at Genome, with collaborators at Tomsk State University, Russia.

1 / 8

Genome-wide gene-family studies number in the thousands, but their methods and results are scattered. I screened four databases under PRISMA and extracted each included study into a structured dataset. Doing that by hand was not realistic, so I wrote the PDF extractor and the PGF-Explorer database listed under Software.

Key findings
  • Crops dominate: wheat (11.9%), rice (5.5%), and tomato (4.8%) lead the primary organisms.
  • WRKY is the most studied family (3.2% of studies).
  • Maximum likelihood is rare: MEGA appears in 91% of studies, IQ-TREE in only 5.8%.
  • The average study team has 7.03 authors.
  • The field's main weaknesses are inconsistent reporting and a lack of methodological standardization, which the proposed pipeline addresses.

Environmental biotechnology

Can plant-made iron nanoparticles make textile wastewater safe for crops?

First author, wet-lab trial plus transcriptomics, under review at Plant, Cell & Environment.

1 / 13

I synthesised iron oxide nanoparticles with Conocarpus erectus leaf extract, compared them with chemically made ones, and used them to treat textile effluent before irrigating wheat. A public cadmium-stress wheat transcriptome then explained, at the gene level, how the plants recovered.

Key findings
  • Leaf-extract nanoparticles outperformed chemical ones, removing 93.9% of COD against 68.1%.
  • Untreated effluent is severely toxic: germination fell to 39% and fresh and dry biomass by about 38 to 43%.
  • Stress triggers active reprogramming, not collapse: upregulated genes outnumber downregulated ones more than two to one.
  • Defence modules: glutathione metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, cell wall remodelling, and metal transporters, centred on GST and NAS genes.
